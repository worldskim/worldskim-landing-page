Europe Wins a Place at the Next Round of Ukraine Talks

Europe will join the next round of talks on ending Russia’s war in Ukraine, U.S. envoys said after meeting Vladimir Putin. The Guardian reports that Jared Kushner said the move would let talks address postwar European security. Putin warned Europe against sending peacekeeping troops and refused to soften his territorial demands.

The BBC reports that Volodymyr Zelensky welcomed the pledge and said it gives the EU a voice. EU leaders meeting in Dublin now face a question over who should speak for them. The New York Times reports that Kushner said naming one European negotiator would be a big achievement. Luxembourg’s Luc Frieden spoke for three Benelux nations in Dublin.

Trump Says the U.S. No Longer Needs a Deal With Iran

President Trump says the United States no longer needs a deal to end its war with Iran. CNBC reports that he wrote, “We’ve got their money” and “we’ve got their Strait”, referring to Hormuz. He told Vice President JD Vance to focus on crime and corruption in Democratic-run cities instead.

The Guardian reports that Vance had reportedly been talking to intermediaries about restarting negotiations. The post effectively normalises a stalemate, with a U.S. blockade on Iranian ports still in place. CNBC reports that Brent crude settled 3.3% higher at $95.82 a barrel after the comments.

The Yen Tests 160 per Dollar as Tokyo Warns It Could Act

The yen briefly touched 160 per dollar on Thursday, near its weakest level since early September. CNBC reports that Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama said the government will respond appropriately to currency volatility. Traders are watching closely for signs that Tokyo will step into the market to support the yen.

The pressure comes from a strong dollar, which is holding near a 19-month high. CNBC reports that traders now see a 14.5% chance of a large Fed rate hike next month. Higher U.S. rates widen the gap with Japan. CNBC reports that the 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.1271% on Friday.

Deep dive

Congo’s Ebola Response Is Losing Money and Ground as the Virus Spreads

What happened. Hundreds of millions of dollars meant to fight Ebola in Congo are not reaching the front line. The BBC reports that MSF says workers are going unpaid and some ambulances have run out of fuel. More than 5,400 people have died since the outbreak was declared in May. It is the second deadliest Ebola outbreak on record.

Where the money went. Africa CDC said last month that $700 million of $962 million disbursed to response groups was unaccounted for. The BBC reports that only 22% of aid agencies have shared details of their work with the health ministry. Health Minister Samuel-Roger Kamba said transparency must be total. MSF wants stronger oversight from the government and UN agencies.

A moving target. The outbreak first centred on a handful of towns in one province. The New York Times reports that it has now crept into eight provinces across an area about the size of Britain. In one besieged town, three quarters of Ebola patients have died. Biakato alone accounted for up to a quarter of new cases nationally.

A new threat. The New York Times reports that the containment campaign has also run into the Islamic State. MSF says close to two thirds of recent deaths occurred in communities rather than treatment centres. The strain has no approved vaccine or treatment. The BBC reports that cases have also been found in Kenya, Uganda and Nigeria.
