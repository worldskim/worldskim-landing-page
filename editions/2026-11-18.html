Europe Pushes Back as the Pentagon Questions Ukraine's Deep Strikes

The Guardian reports Pentagon policy chief Elbridge Colby told Ukraine's backers that deep strikes inside Russia are counterproductive. He spoke on Tuesday at Nato headquarters in Brussels. Colby said the strikes had not been effective in ending the war. He urged allies to focus support on frontline equipment and midrange strikes. German defence minister Boris Pistorius said criticising such strikes was "quite a stupid thing."

British defence minister Wes Streeting said the strikes were saving lives. Meanwhile Russian attacks are hitting Kyiv hard. The Guardian reports many homes in the capital now have power for only four hours a day. A district head said some homes had outages of up to 37 hours straight. Volodymyr Zelenskyy has blamed Mayor Vitali Klitschko for failing to prepare for winter.

UK Inflation Rises to 3.6% as Record Diesel Prices Bite

The Office for National Statistics said consumer price inflation rose to 3.6% in October, from 3.5% in September. The Guardian reports diesel averaged 199.5p a litre, the highest on records dating back to 1996. Petrol rose to 174.7p a litre. Motor fuel prices were up 33.2% on a year earlier. The Iran war has pushed up oil costs.

The BBC reports consultancy Cornwall Insight forecasts energy bills will rise by £443 a year from January. That would take a typical household bill to £2,166, a 26% jump. Regulator Ofgem will announce the new price cap on Nov. 25. The Bank of England raised interest rates from 3.75% to 4% earlier this month. The government plans a £100 payment to 20 million homes in January.

Lammy Urges a Rejoin Pledge as Britain Heads to an EU Summit

The Guardian reports former foreign secretary David Lammy urged Prime Minister Andy Burnham to campaign on rejoining the EU. Lammy wrote in the Times that any deal should then be put to the people. Burnham will attend a UK-EU summit in Brussels on Friday. He has said he will stick to Labour's 2024 manifesto red lines before an election.

BBC Europe editor Katya Adler reports the summit agenda is limited. It includes a youth work and mobility scheme, carbon pricing and a deal to ease food trade. The Guardian reports the CBI wants Brexit limits on business travel eased urgently. The group's Sean McGuire said practically nothing had been achieved on business mobility.

Deep dive

A Killing Near Jericho as Allies Target West Bank Settlements

The BBC reports a Palestinian woman, 72, was shot dead in al-Duyuk al-Tahta, near Jericho, according to the Palestinian health ministry. Several other people were wounded on Monday night. Accounts conflict. Local Palestinians said settlers attacked. The Israeli army said Palestinians rammed an Israeli vehicle and threw rocks, and Israelis opened fire. It did not mention the woman's death.

The Guardian reports dozens of settlers moved into eight buildings in the village two weeks ago. Residents said the move was backed by the settler group Nachala. Witnesses said six men were wounded by gunfire. The woman's son was hurt after colliding with a parked settler car. His wife said soldiers later arrested him.

The BBC reports the UK sanctioned eight construction and property firms over settlement building. The measures also target Tzav 9, a group the UK says blocked aid to Gaza. Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Saar called the sanctions a hostile act. He said Israel would respond with countermeasures. The Palestinian foreign ministry welcomed the move and urged others to follow.

The Guardian reports Australia also sanctioned three individuals and four organisations, including Daniella Weiss and Nachala. Foreign Minister Penny Wong said settlement expansion and settler violence are extinguishing the possibility of a two-state solution. The government said its sanctions were coordinated with the UK. The BBC reports the UK move came two days before a deadline for E1 construction tender bids.
