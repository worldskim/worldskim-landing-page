New Zealand’s Close Count Puts Coalition Arithmetic in Focus

New Zealand’s National and Labour parties were close in Saturday’s preliminary election count. Reuters reported a narrow National lead as the first results arrived. In later reporting checked at 11:14:36 UTC, the governing coalition retained a slim projected advantage. No final result or government agreement was established.

Al Jazeera reports that the centrist Opportunity Party crossed the 5% threshold in preliminary results. Its performance could matter if coalition negotiations are required. The election focused heavily on living costs, including grocery prices. Final results, including overseas ballots, are expected Nov. 27, so the seat picture remains provisional.

Qatar Says Iran Mediation Continues After New U.S. Strikes

Qatar said efforts to contain the Iran conflict remained active Saturday, rejecting reports that negotiations had collapsed. Al Jazeera reports that its foreign ministry disputed those claims. The statement followed Trump’s confirmation of new U.S. strikes against Iranian air defenses and Iranian reports of explosions on Qeshm Island.

The island sits beside the Strait of Hormuz, an important route for global oil shipments. AP reports that Trump offered limited detail about the latest target. Gulf governments have continued diplomatic calls. Public claims of progress should not be confused with a negotiated settlement, which has not been announced.

U.S. Hiring Improves While Canada’s Labor Market Weakens

New employment figures show contrasting conditions in North America. The United States added 165,000 jobs in October and unemployment fell to 4.1%. Reuters reports that earlier U.S. hiring totals were also revised upward. The stronger figures leave the Federal Reserve weighing labor market resilience against inflation and household financial pressure.

Canada, meanwhile, lost a net 4,700 jobs and its unemployment rate rose to 6.7%. Reuters reports weakness in private sector and full time employment. Canada’s economy had lost 46,000 jobs overall this year. The separate U.S. report showed more people entering the labor force, despite its lower unemployment rate.

Deep dive

Kyiv’s New Infrastructure Damage Tests the Space for Talks

Russian attacks hit Kyiv and surrounding areas overnight before another planned round of talks. Reuters reports damage to residential buildings and a kindergarten building, citing Ukrainian officials. Subsequent reporting described strikes affecting a major bridge and energy infrastructure. The attacks continued despite diplomatic preparations, leaving civilian protection an immediate concern separate from negotiations over a settlement.

The practical effects extend beyond individual impact sites. DW reports that Kyiv’s mayor described serious difficulties with electricity, heating and water. Overnight temperatures fell to freezing. Damage to energy systems can therefore affect households far from a strike location. Ukrainian officials have appealed for more support as they prepare for further attacks on infrastructure.

Russia’s Defense Ministry said the bridge carried weapons and that a targeted plant produced missiles. DW attributes those descriptions to Moscow; they are not independent verification of the targets’ use. Al Jazeera reports that Ukrainian officials described damage to civilian buildings. The competing accounts require clear attribution rather than treating either side’s claims as an established account.

Negotiators from Ukraine, Russia and the United States are expected in Abu Dhabi Tuesday. Al Jazeera reports that Ukraine is seeking concrete security guarantees from its partners. Talks among allies in Brussels also addressed reconstruction and funding for next year. DW reports that territorial and security questions have prevented earlier rounds from achieving a breakthrough.
