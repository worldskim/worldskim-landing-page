Explosion Near a School in Eastern Afghanistan Kills Seven

The BBC reports at least seven students were killed and 99 injured in an explosion near a school in Afghanistan on Saturday morning. It happened in the Anderseir area of Asadabad, capital of Kunar province. A Taliban interior ministry spokesperson said initial investigations blamed negligence at a mining companies' explosives depot.

AP quotes Kunar's culture and information director as saying the blast appeared to originate in a neighboring explosives depot. Up to 15 of the wounded remain in hospital, he said, while the rest were discharged. Police said the explosion occurred at 10 a.m. AP notes safety standards at Afghan mining companies are often poor.

MSF Says an Israeli Strike Killed Two Staff at Its Gaza Clinic

Medecins Sans Frontieres says an Israeli strike on one of its Gaza clinics killed two colleagues, both watchmen. The charity said it was a direct hit and brings its staff killed in Gaza to 17. It said the premises were clearly marked and their location was known to the Israeli military.

The BBC reports the Israeli military said the two men were Hamas operatives acting as security guards. The IDF said the strike was not directed at a hospital and caused no damage to medical infrastructure. WHO chief Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus called attacks on health facilities and workers in Gaza outrageous and unacceptable.

Spain Votes Sunday With the Far Right in Reach of Power

The BBC reports Spain's main party leaders held final rallies on Friday ahead of Sunday's election. Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez said he was confident of defying the polls. Alberto Nunez Feijoo's People's Party is narrowly ahead. Almost 39 million people have a vote, and no polls have been published since Monday.

CNBC reports the snap election could end Sanchez's eight years in power. It could also put the far right in government for the first time in Spain's modern democratic era. The People's Party could win a majority with Vox, according to El Pais polling averages. Housing and the cost of living have dominated the campaign.

How the Iran War Is Creating Winners and Losers in Fuel

CNBC reports Dangote Petroleum Refinery is expected to begin trading in Lagos on Dec. 1, according to multiple media reports. Reuters calculations put its IPO-implied valuation at about $47 billion. That would make it the biggest stock market listing Africa has ever seen. CNBC says the offer was reportedly heavily oversubscribed.

The war helps explain the appetite. CNBC says the refinery has emerged as a key jet fuel provider in recent months. Buyers pivoted to West Africa amid Middle Eastern supply disruptions due to the Iran war, CNBC reports. Its refined-product loadings hit a record 575,000 barrels per day through the first three weeks of November.

For Saudi Arabia, CNBC reports, the picture is harsher. Real GDP contracted 3.8% year over year in the third quarter as oil activity fell 20.4%. Houthi strikes have hit targets including Riyadh Airport and the country's largest oil refinery. Economy Minister Faisal Alibrahim told CNBC spending is shifting to delivery at the right cost.

Airlines are among the losers. CNBC reports budget carrier Norse Atlantic filed for bankruptcy on Friday after failing to secure funding. It said soaring fuel costs had strained operations throughout the year. Its chief executive told CNBC in May it had canceled over 700 flights since the start of the Iran war.
