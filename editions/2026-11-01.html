01The big read

The AI money pit just gave Wall Street its worst day since June

The S&P 500 fell 1.8% on Friday and the Nasdaq dropped 2.9%, the S&P's worst day since June, AP reports. Intel plunged 28.6%, its worst drop since 1974, after a weak forecast, and Nvidia fell 4.2%.

The fear is that Big Tech's huge AI bills won't pay off soon enough. A day earlier, Alphabet had its worst day on record. It reported negative free cash flow of $9.1 billion and plans to spend up to $205 billion this year, Reuters reports.

Spain's train crash killed 69, and officials are pointing at the control room

At least 69 people died when a Renfe train hit a stopped Iryo train near Urda, on the Madrid to Seville high speed line. Transport Minister Óscar Puente called the control center's order to let trains resume "inexplicable," ABC News reports.

Unions say controllers are overloaded and the 30 year old LZB safety system should have been replaced, AP reports. It is Spain's second deadly rail crash this year, and Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez has called early elections for Nov. 29.

Riot police, a suspended judge and a shove: Poland's court fight turns physical

Judges chosen by Poland's parliament entered the Constitutional Tribunal on Friday and suspended its president, Bogdan Swieczkowski, as riot police guarded the building. President Karol Nawrocki, a Swieczkowski ally, turned his plane around on the way to Senegal.

Around 1 a.m., video showed Nawrocki push past a judge to enter the court. Prime Minister Donald Tusk wrote that "restoring the rule of law is our obligation," as hundreds protested for a second night, AP reports.

World view

Israeli strikes killed at least eight people in Gaza on Sunday, including five police officers in Khan Younis, medics and Gaza officials said.

Interest is growing in a limited Black Sea ceasefire to keep grain and energy ships moving, Secretary of State Marco Rubio said after a G20 foreign ministers' meeting ended without a joint statement.

Mass protests swept more than 40 Italian cities during a 24 hour general strike over pay, living costs and military spending.

Thousands gathered in Trappes, outside Paris, to honor the victims of a shooting whose suspect admitted targeting Muslim and African people, prosecutors said.

ExxonMobil and Chevron rose after beating profit forecasts, as Brent crude climbed 2.4% to $113.43 a barrel.

Deep dive

El Salvador rushed 2,000 troops into Haiti. The rules they fight by are still unclear

El Salvador now has about 2,000 soldiers in Haiti, and sources say the force will be capped at 2,500 for now, Reuters reports. President Nayib Bukele says his government is covering 90% of the cost. The surge matched in 10 days what a UN backed force struggled to deploy in more than a year.

The trigger was an Oct. 18 ambush. Gangs fired on an armored vehicle near Port au Prince, and four Salvadoran aviation technicians fled the burning vehicle and went missing. They belong to the UN backed Gang Suppression Force. Haiti's government says they were abducted, though no group has claimed responsibility.

Bukele calls it a rescue, writing that it is "not an operation against the Haitian people", AP reports. But officials have not explained the troops' command structure or rules of engagement. There is no formal agreement or UN backing for the surge, though a U.S. official said Pentagon cargo planes helped move the troops.

Gangs reportedly control about 70% of Port au Prince, and violence has displaced nearly 1.5 million Haitians. Residents have reported heavy gunfire and drone strikes. Sam Vigersky of the Council on Foreign Relations said the picture points "towards one of escalation." At home, the mission is popular: a CID Gallup poll found 86% of Salvadorans approve.
