Russia Hits Three Kyiv Bridges in One of the War's Largest Air Attacks

The BBC reports ballistic missiles hit three bridges over the Dnipro River in Kyiv overnight. The BBC says eight people were killed, six of them in the capital. Ukraine's air force says Russia launched almost 100 missiles and around 400 drones. The BBC says the attack came days after Ukraine damaged the Crimean Bridge.

Politico reports Poland's prime minister Donald Tusk was briefed on a violation of Polish airspace. Rzeszow airport suspended flights, the outlet says. Politico cites lower early tolls, including at least five dead. Kyiv's mayor Vitali Klitschko urged residents not to drive into the city. Putin had vowed a response far more painful than Ukraine's bridge strike.

Venezuela Signs a Military Cooperation Deal With the United States

The BBC reports Venezuela's interim government signed a military cooperation agreement with US defence secretary Pete Hegseth. The deal was signed on Wednesday in Caracas. Few details have been released, the BBC says. Military ties were cut in 2005 by Hugo Chavez. Interim President Delcy Rodriguez said the deal would help confront common threats.

The Guardian reports Hegseth used the visit to boast of Nicolas Maduro's capture by US forces. Maduro was seized on Jan. 3 and is in custody in New York. He and his wife deny the charges, the BBC says. Hegseth said he looks forward to unlocking Venezuela's resources together.

SoftBank Leads an AI Sell-Off Across Asia's Tech Stocks

CNBC reports SoftBank Group shares closed 14.48% lower on Friday in Tokyo. It was the stock's worst day since August 2024. CNBC says the sell-off swept Asian tech stocks after Anthropic's market debut. South Korea's Kospi ended 5.21% lower, while TSMC closed 4.76% lower in Taiwan.

CNBC reports Anthropic was down 6.37% in premarket trading at 5 a.m. ET on Friday. Nvidia was down 2.67% and AMD fell 5.43% at that time. Anthropic had closed at $97.17 on Thursday after its debut. US markets open at 9:30 a.m. ET.

After Eight Years in Space, BepiColombo Prepares to Orbit Mercury

The BBC reports the BepiColombo spacecraft is about to get into orbit around Mercury. It has travelled almost six billion miles over eight years. The mission is a collaboration between the European and Japanese space agencies. It launched from French Guiana in 2018. A propulsion problem added a year to the journey, the BBC says.

The Guardian reports flight controllers will fire the thrusters on Saturday to send it into orbit. It would become only the second mission to circle the planet. The paper puts the mission's cost at EUR 1.6bn. Scientists hope to learn why Mercury has a magnetic field and an unusually large core.

ESA says four thrusters will slow the spacecraft as it approaches Mercury's south pole. It will first enter an elliptical orbit of 590 by 11,640 km. The two orbiters are due to separate on Dec. 9-10. Science operations for both spacecraft are set to begin in April 2027, ESA says.

The BBC says the final manoeuvre is perilous, a 37-minute turn to be grabbed by gravity. If anything goes wrong, the craft could hurtle past Mercury. Only two Nasa missions have visited before. The BBC says scientists also want to explain ice in polar craters and strange young hollows on the surface.

How long is a single day on Mercury, measured in Earth days, according to the BBC?

The answer is 59. The BBC reports a Mercury year lasts just 88 Earth days but a day lasts 59.
