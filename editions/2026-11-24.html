Hasina Says She Plans to Return to Bangladesh on December 16

The BBC reports former Bangladesh prime minister Sheikh Hasina says she plans to return home on 16 December. She has lived in exile in Delhi since August 2024. A Bangladeshi court sentenced her to death in absentia last year. She denies the charges. State minister Humayun Kabir said she would be brought back in handcuffs.

CNBC reports a US panel says China is actively working to suppress India's rise. The commission's Nov. 18 report cited pressure on electronics, drug inputs and battery technology. Hasina's political activities from India have also complicated Delhi's efforts to rebuild ties with Dhaka, the BBC says. India's foreign ministry did not immediately respond to the BBC.

UK Sanctions Myanmar's Generals After the Genocide Ruling

The BBC reports the UK has sanctioned five senior Myanmar military leaders. It follows an International Court of Justice ruling that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya. It is the first time a state has been found guilty of the crime. Australia, Belgium, Portugal, Spain and Canada also acknowledged the ruling, the BBC says.

The UK government says it formally recognised the genocide on 23 November and announced new measures against the Myanmar military. It plans to target the export and supply of aviation fuel to Myanmar. It pledged 5 million pounds in aid for Rohingya in Rakhine State and up to 3 million pounds for justice.

France Flashes Recession Warnings as Sanofi Agrees a Big Deal

CNBC reports French business activity fell to a six-month low in November, according to an S&P Global survey published Monday. Companies cut jobs for a seventh straight month. Firms are delaying decisions ahead of the April 2027 presidential election. Bank of France Governor Emmanuel Moulin still expects growth in the fourth quarter.

CNBC reports French drugmaker Sanofi agreed to buy Insmed for $41 billion, or $175 per share in cash. If closed, it would be the industry's biggest deal since December 2023. Insmed shares rallied 14% in premarket trading on Tuesday. Sanofi stock rose 6% in Paris, CNBC says.

Why Iran's Shadow Banks Have Become a Main US Target

CNBC reports Iran's Revolutionary Guards said three oil tankers caught fire after hitting naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz. The Guards said all three were under US military escort. Iran has warned ships to use only the northern lane approved by Tehran. The US has a third aircraft carrier in the Gulf enforcing a blockade.

The US Treasury said on Monday it sanctioned over two dozen Iranian exchange houses, front companies and key personnel. It called the action a sweeping effort to disrupt Iran's shadow banking networks. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Iran's economy is underwater. The move is part of Operation Economic Outcast, which Bessent announced on August 24.

A Treasury unit, FinCEN, said it identified about $14.7 billion of activity potentially linked to Iranian shadow banking. The figure covers January 2025 to August 2026. The most common flagged route ran from mainland China-based banks to UAE-based banks, about $2.6 billion. Networks also run through Hong Kong and Singapore, it said.

The pressure is not only financial. CNBC cites a Wall Street Journal report that Washington is investigating Iran as a primary suspect in cyberattacks on tankers. As many as 50 ships were believed targeted, the Journal reported. Russia was also being examined. Brent futures dipped to $89.2 a barrel on Tuesday, CNBC says.
