America votes today, and Kyiv and NATO are holding their breath

Americans vote today in midterm elections that will decide control of Congress, and the results will be read closely far beyond U.S. borders.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned that Russia could launch a massive attack on energy and logistics targets around the election. And NATO diplomats fear President Trump could step up his attacks on the alliance if Republicans lose.

Yemen's Houthis are now 50 miles from Aden, closer than any time since 2015

Houthi forces have advanced to about 50 miles (80 km) from Aden, the seat of Yemen's internationally recognized government, closer than at any point since 2015, Reuters reports, citing sources.

Iran's foreign minister said talks with Saudi Arabia about the fighting are ongoing. Days earlier, the UN demanded an investigation after a Yemeni UN staff member died in Houthi custody.

Europe is rehearsing how to send troops to Ukraine after a peace deal

The UK and France will lead an exercise in Poland with nearly 3,000 personnel from 12 countries to test how forces could deploy to Ukraine under a possible peace deal, the BBC reports.

U.S., Russian and Ukrainian officials are set to hold talks in Abu Dhabi on Nov. 10 and 11, and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said Zelenskyy will visit Berlin as a show of support.

Deep dive

Britain wants to apologize for the slave trade. It still won't pay reparations

King Charles III and Prime Minister Andy Burnham are in Antigua for the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting, where leaders from 56 countries are gathering, the BBC reports.

Burnham is opening talks about the UK issuing a formal apology for the slave trade, possibly next year, when Britain holds the rotating G20 presidency. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said the government is open to it.

London's line on money has not changed. Miliband said financial reparations are not "the right or feasible answer." Instead, Burnham is offering what Downing Street calls a debt reset for Commonwealth countries struggling with the rising cost of interest payments.

The issue clouded the last summit in Samoa two years ago, when the previous government said reparations were not on the agenda. Burnham wrote in the Guardian that avoiding the conversation had hurt the Commonwealth. Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch warned that an apology would open the door to demands for cash.
