America Votes Today, and Kyiv and NATO Are Holding Their Breath

Americans vote today in midterm elections that will decide control of Congress. Abroad, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned that Russia could launch a massive attack on energy and logistics targets around the election, The Hill reports. And NATO diplomats fear President Trump could step up his attacks on the alliance if Republicans lose, according to Politico.

Sources: The Hill · Politico

Yemen's Houthis Are Now 50 Miles From Aden, Closer Than Any Time Since 2015

Houthi forces have advanced to about 50 miles (80 km) from Aden, the seat of Yemen's internationally recognized government, closer than at any point since 2015, Reuters reports, citing sources. A push on Aden would reopen one of the region's most dangerous front lines.

Sources: Reuters

Europe Is Rehearsing How to Send Troops to Ukraine After a Peace Deal

The UK and France will lead an exercise in Poland with nearly 3,000 personnel from 12 countries to test how forces could deploy to Ukraine under a possible peace deal, the BBC reports. Separately, U.S., Russian and Ukrainian officials are set to hold talks in Abu Dhabi on Nov. 10 and 11, Axios reports.

Sources: BBC · Axios

Supermarket Fridges Went Dark in Six EU Countries. Brussels Wants Answers

Refrigerators in supermarkets across six EU countries went offline in a cyberattack, and the European Commission says it will now assess how prepared supermarkets are, Politico reports. It's a reminder that everyday infrastructure, down to the dairy aisle, is now a target.

Sources: Politico

King Charles Is in Antigua, and an Apology for Slavery Is on the Table

King Charles III and UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham are in Antigua for Commonwealth talks that will cover a possible UK apology for the transatlantic slave trade and a "debt reset," the BBC reports. Burnham has said he is open to a formal apology.

Sources: BBC: Antigua talks · BBC: apology
