Russia and Ukraine Agree a Prisoner Exchange but Report No Major Peace Breakthrough

Russia and Ukraine agreed to a large prisoner exchange Wednesday as talks in Abu Dhabi ended without a major peace breakthrough. AP reports both delegations confirmed the planned swap. Russia’s negotiator described the agreement as in principle. The number of prisoners and practical arrangements had not been finalized.

The humanitarian agreement did not settle disputes over territory or security guarantees. AP reports U.S. envoys planned separate meetings with Vladimir Putin and Volodymyr Zelenskyy. European participation remained contested, with Moscow opposing a direct role. The talks were the delegations’ first meeting in 9 months, while missile and drone attacks continued.

Climate Leaders Warn That War Cannot Put Emissions Action on Hold

World leaders began two days of COP31 speeches in Antalya on Wednesday. Reuters reports the summit seeks progress despite wars, inflation and other competing crises. U.N. Secretary General António Guterres said the climate crisis was outrunning the international response. Turkey wants electrification at the centre of a final agreement.

The proposal would raise electricity’s share of global energy use to 35% by 2035. Reuters reports critics want a clearer plan to reduce fossil fuel use. The United States stayed away for a second year. Barbados’s Mia Mottley called for cheaper finance and disaster clauses in international loans, highlighting vulnerable countries’ demands.

Indonesia’s Lawmakers Prepare a Motion as Gibran’s Resignation Plans Advance

Indonesia’s vice presidential crisis advanced on Wednesday as lawmakers prepared a parliamentary motion. Antara reports Deputy Speaker Saan Mustopa planned to submit it to Thursday’s plenary. The proposal had support from 120 legislators. Projo leader Budi Arie Setiadi also confirmed Gibran Rakabuming Raka’s resignation plans when contacted by the agency.

Formal completion remained distinct from those statements at this edition’s cutoff. The palace had confirmed Gibran’s intention to step down on Tuesday, following a meeting with President Prabowo Subianto. The dispute concerned his educational qualifications. A Supreme Court ruling invalidated his equivalency certificate, while Gibran sought another opportunity to defend his diplomas in court.

How an Alleged Fairford Plot Tested Security Around a U.S. Bomber Base

What happened. U.S. prosecutors unsealed charges Tuesday against three men accused of an international attack campaign. NPR reports the indictment includes a September plot against RAF Fairford, a British base hosting American bombers. Prosecutors linked the defendants to Iran’s Revolutionary Guards and Kata’ib Hezbollah. The charges describe allegations, not findings of guilt.

Why it matters. Fairford serves as a forward operating base for U.S. strategic bombers. NPR reports associates allegedly filmed its perimeter and obtained vans and fuel drums. Prosecutors say the vehicles carried false commercial branding. Two were stopped near the base at a security roadblock, and authorities seized all three the following morning.

What each side says. The indictment alleges the network continued operating after one defendant entered U.S. custody in May. The Guardian reports prosecutors say coded family calls transmitted instructions. The alleged preparations included researching chemical accelerants and metal objects for shrapnel. The Fairford allegations form part of a broader case involving attacks on Jewish communities.

What comes next. The criminal case must test prosecutors’ account rather than treating the indictment as a verdict. NPR notes British authorities had separately charged two men over the incident. Neither government confirmed a reported recent aircraft relocation was caused by an Iranian threat. Britain’s defence ministry said aircraft routinely move for operational reasons.
