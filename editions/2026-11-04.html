Iran Reads America’s Midterm Vote as a Rejection of War

Iran’s chief negotiator said Americans had voted against war after the U.S. midterm elections. BBC News reports Mohammed Bagher Ghalibaf endorsed that message on Wednesday. President Donald Trump acknowledged the Iran conflict and higher petrol prices hurt Republicans. He nevertheless said he did not regret launching the joint attack with Israel.

The response did not establish a diplomatic breakthrough. BBC News reports Iran’s supreme leader said U.S. hostility would not change. Indirect talks remained stalled, with Qatar mediating, while the Strait of Hormuz stayed effectively closed, disrupting an important global energy route. Neither the election result nor Tehran’s interpretation ended the conflict.

South Africans Vote Amid Anger Over Water and Electricity Failures

South Africans voted in municipal elections Wednesday amid frustration with water outages, power cuts and failing infrastructure. The Guardian reports Johannesburg residents described repeated service breakdowns. The African National Congress faced pressure from several rivals. Polling suggested losses, but those forecasts were not final results or an announcement of who would govern.

The vote chooses councillors rather than directly electing mayors. The Guardian reports parties may need coalition negotiations after the count. Johannesburg had already cycled through 10 mayors in as many years, with unstable local administrations complicating service delivery. Election officials had a week to announce final results before councils moved to select their leadership.

Zelenskyy Reports 40 Confirmed Dead in the Komyshuvakha Strike

At 08:44 EST Wednesday, The Guardian reported Volodymyr Zelenskyy’s update that 40 people were confirmed dead after Monday’s Komyshuvakha school strike. His account put more than 30 people still missing. The building had served as an aid distribution site. Rescue operations continued as officials searched the rubble.

The revised toll followed earlier reports of 31 and then 33 deaths. The Guardian’s morning briefing describes Russian strikes on energy infrastructure alongside the rescue effort. Power stations and substations were hit across Ukraine. Diplomatic preparations continued for talks in Abu Dhabi, but those plans did not halt attacks or resolve the humanitarian emergency.

Deep dive

A Cyberattack on French Blood Storage Shows the Stakes of Connected Equipment

What happened. France’s national blood service said hackers raised temperatures at three cold stores containing blood products. The Guardian reports alarms allowed technicians to intervene before the products were compromised. The attack occurred overnight Monday and affected facilities run by contractors. Stored products, including plasma bags, were moved to other sites as a precaution.

Why it matters. Blood products depend on controlled storage conditions before reaching patients. The Guardian reports the French service supplies more than 1,900 hospitals. The incident followed refrigeration attacks on supermarkets in several European countries. Connected control systems let operators monitor equipment remotely, but unpatched vulnerabilities can also expose those systems to hostile interference.

What each side says. The blood service said it had alerted France’s cybersecurity agency and health ministry. The Guardian reports European authorities were discussing a collective response. No motive was known and nobody had claimed responsibility. Speculation about a state actor or an individual hacker remained speculation, not an established attribution of the attacks.

What comes next. The service said monitoring had been reinforced across its equipment. The Guardian reports the affected blood products had been relocated. Investigators still needed to establish responsibility and whether incidents shared a cause. Wednesday’s reporting showed successful intervention at these stores, not proof that all connected refrigeration systems were secure against future attacks.
