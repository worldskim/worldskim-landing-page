Solomon Islands Drops a China Pact for a Treaty With Australia

The BBC reports Solomon Islands signed a treaty with Australia in Canberra on Wednesday. It follows reports that Honiara told Beijing it would not renew a 2022 security pact. Prime Minister Matthew Wale said there were "ongoing discussions" with China. He said a non-disclosure agreement stopped him discussing the pact.

A joint statement from both prime ministers says the treaty reflects the choice of Australia as primary security partner. Australia will provide budget support and help fund free education, roads and renewable energy. Solomon Islanders will get longer visa stays. On Tuesday, China's foreign ministry neither confirmed nor denied the pact had ended, the BBC says.

Pakistan's First Astronaut Joins a Chinese Space Mission

The BBC reports China launched Shenzhou-24 on Wednesday with Pakistan's Muhammad Zeeshan Ali aboard. He is the first foreigner to join a Chinese crewed mission. The rocket lifted off at 00:18 GMT from the Gobi desert. Ali, a former fighter pilot, will return after a short stay. Two Chinese crewmates will spend six months aboard.

Reuters reports Ali became the first foreigner to board China's Tiangong space station. China is promoting Tiangong as a hub for international cooperation. The NASA-led International Space Station faces possible retirement by 2030. A Pakistani-built rover is also set to fly on a Chinese lunar mission around 2028, Reuters says.

A US Jury Convicts Huawei but Clears It of Racketeering

The BBC reports a Brooklyn jury found Huawei guilty on 10 of 12 counts on Tuesday. They included conspiracy to steal trade secrets, bank fraud and conspiracy to violate US sanctions. Jurors acquitted the Chinese firm of racketeering conspiracy. China's government criticised what it called US "suppression" of Chinese companies.

The Justice Department says Huawei lied to banks about its business in Iran. It says Huawei supplied surveillance cameras that helped Iranian authorities monitor protests. CNBC reports sentencing is scheduled for September 2027. Huawei called the remaining charges "without legal or factual merit" and said it would consider further legal action.

Why the Houthis Are Taking Their War to Saudi Cities

CNBC reports a Houthi attack hit the Public Investment Fund tower in Riyadh on Tuesday, injuring 11 people. The injured included Saudi, Indian, Nepali and Egyptian nationals. It was the second attack on the tower in six days. A coalition spokesman called it a "cowardly terrorist attack" and said investigators were at the scene.

BBC Arabic reports the Houthis said on Wednesday they targeted the Ras Tanura refinery and a financial centre in Riyadh. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the refinery strike started fires. The Saudi-led coalition said its air defences destroyed four drones over Ras Tanura. It reported falling debris but no injuries in an initial assessment.

The threats are widening. CNBC reports Saree warned on Wednesday that Jeddah and all Saudi Red Sea ports would become targets if Riyadh attacks Hodeidah. The coalition had asked civilians to leave that Yemeni port on Nov. 20. The Houthis have also fired drones and missiles at Saudi airports, according to reports cited by CNBC.

Gulf economies are still absorbing the wider Iran war. CNBC reports the IMF now expects the UAE economy to grow 0.2% in 2026, revised from a 3.9% contraction forecast a month earlier. The UAE economy minister told CNBC the country is "very optimistic" about 7%-8% growth next year. Qatar and Pakistan condemned the Riyadh tower attack, BBC Arabic says.
