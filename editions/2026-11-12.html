Gibran Submits His Resignation as Indonesia’s Vice President

Gibran Rakabuming Raka submitted his resignation to Indonesia’s parliamentary assembly leaders on Thursday. Antara reports Speaker Ahmad Muzani read the letter to journalists after a 20 minute meeting. The letter gave no specific reason. Gibran said he acted voluntarily for the nation and the smooth functioning of government.

The assembly speaker said the vice presidential post was now vacant. Antara reports the assembly would hold a joint session and notify President Prabowo Subianto. Muzani said a 14 day nomination period would follow that notification. The formal letter follows Tuesday’s stated intention to resign amid a dispute over Gibran’s educational qualifications.

Witkoff Says Another Ukraine Talks Round Is Agreed After Meeting Putin

U.S. envoys completed their meeting with Vladimir Putin in Moscow on Thursday. The Guardian’s dated live coverage reports Steve Witkoff said another trilateral round was agreed. He said Europeans would join the next meeting. Witkoff and Jared Kushner then left Moscow en route to Dublin for consultations with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy.

The agreement to meet again was not a peace settlement. The Guardian reports Putin repeated demands over occupied territory and opposed foreign peacekeepers. The Kremlin called the talks useful and said the process would continue. ABC News describes unresolved differences over territory and security guarantees after the earlier Abu Dhabi discussions.

Iran Warns of Wider Retaliation as Israeli Strike Preparations Are Reported

Iran warned it could target bases beyond the region if the United States launched another war. Al Jazeera attributes the warning to security chief Mohsen Rezaei. The report followed Israeli broadcaster KAN’s account of military preparations for a possible attack. Those reported directives did not establish that any attack had begun.

The preparations also exposed a dispute within Israeli politics. Al Jazeera reports Gadi Eisenkot urged caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to avoid unnecessary operations. Eisenkot is trying to form the next government. Washington continued to emphasize economic pressure and diplomacy, while Tehran said trust remained an obstacle to agreement over the Strait of Hormuz.

Spain’s First General Strike in 14 Years Puts Housing at the Centre of the Election

What happened. Spain held a general strike over housing costs on Wednesday, its first in 14 years. BBC News reports large marches in Madrid and Barcelona. Some Barcelona protesters set bins alight and smashed windows, and police used tear gas. The housing demonstrations came less than 3 weeks before a snap national election.

Why it matters. Rising rents have put housing at the centre of the campaign. BBC News reports a 50 square metre Madrid flat averaged 1,170 euros monthly in October, citing Idealista. That figure describes advertised local rents, not every tenant’s bill. The strike brought tenants’ groups and workers’ unions together around demands for more affordable homes.

What each side says. Sánchez and his ministers encouraged workers to strike for affordable housing. BBC News reports the opposition People’s Party accused the government of exploiting the action for electoral gain. Union leaders described participation as strong, while a business association called the protest political. Parliament’s failure to approve housing reform had prompted the snap election.

What comes next. Spaniards vote on 29 November, with housing costs now a central campaign issue. BBC News reports transport and other public services faced serious disruption during the strike. Its reporting described dozens of arrests, not a final nationwide count. Participation figures and Wednesday’s clashes did not determine the election’s outcome or settle the housing policy dispute.
